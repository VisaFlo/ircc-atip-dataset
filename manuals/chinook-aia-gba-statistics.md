MEMORANDUM TO THE DEPUTY MINISTER AND THE ASSOCIATE DEPUTY MINISTER

ALGORITHMIC IMPACT ASSESSMENT FOR THE CHINOOK PROCESSING AID

FOR APPROVAL

SUMMARY

The purpose of this memorandum is to seek your approval of the final results of the Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) for Chinook, as well as your approval to publish the results of the AIA on the Open Government Portal.

- The AIA is a mandatory impact assessment tool under the Treasury Board Directive on Automated Decision-Making. It determines the level of impact of automated decision systems on clients and prescribes requirements to mitigate risks. Although Chinook does not include any automated decision-making functionality, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) agreed to conduct an AIA in response to recommendations from the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Citizenship and Immigration (CIMM) in its report on Differential Treatment in Recruitment and Acceptance Rates of Foreign Students in Quebec and in the Rest of Canada.
- We recommend that you approve publication of the AIA by checking the “I concur" box and signing this memorandum by
BACKGROUND:

In response to growing volumes and inventories, as well as various Global Case Management System (GCMS) and information technology limitations, an early version of Chinook was developed in Excel and deployed in a limited capacity in 2018. Chinook displays information stored in GCMS in a more user-friendly way, allowing for increased GCMS user productivity. It does not utilize artificial intelligence (AI), nor advanced analytics for decision-making. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) officers who use Chinook make all their own final decisions, and Chinook does not make recommendations to officers.

- Use of Chinook was mentioned in a 2021 affidavit related to Abigail Ocran v. The Minister of Citizenship and Immigration. IRCC has received criticism for the use of this tool from the legal community, Parliamentarians and other stakeholders. Criticism surrounds a perceived lack of transparency, an observed increase in refusal rates which coincided with increased Chinook usage, and false narratives about Chinook being an automated decision tool powered by AI.
- IRCC has heard calls from various sources to publish additional information about Chinook. CIMM report 8¹, published in May 2022, recommended that IRCC complete an AIA, as well as a Gender-Based Analysis (GBA) Plus for Chinook (recommendations 25 and 24 respectively). Given the basic functionalities of the tool, and the fact that it does not make or recommend decisions, IRCC's position is that Chinook does not meet the definition of an automated decision system set out in the Treasury Board Directive on Automated Decision-Making, the policy instrument which mandates the completion of an AIA for tools within scope.
- The Directive outlines requirements to ensure the responsible use of automated decision systems to support administrative decision-making. The centrepiece of the Directive is the AIA, which is a questionnaire that helps to assess the overall impact that a system can have on clients. It then prescribes requirements to mitigate risks commensurate with the impact level. According to the Directive, responsibility for ensuring that requirements are met is established at the Assistant Deputy Minister level. This includes the completion, approval and publication of the AIA. Given the sensitivity around this file, the Department has elected to solicit approval to publish the AIA at the Deputy Minister level.

CURRENT STATUS:

- The AIA questionnaire is composed of 51 risk and 34 mitigation questions. The Department's responses for Chinook are included in Annexes A and B, with additional supplementary details provided in Annexes C and D. Given that Chinook does not make any recommendations or decisions, nor does it exercise logic or incorporate algorithms for decision-making, many of the questions in the AIA either do not apply, or require further explanation, which is not possible directly within the fixed parameters of the online assessment tool. Accordingly, the Department has prepared supplementary information which will be published as a companion to the AIA. Treasury Board Secretariat (TBS) has been briefed and is supportive of this approach.
- TBS’ AIA tool automatically determines the level of impact of an automated decision system.

- In addition to the Directive requirements, a GBA Plus assessment is in the process of being completed to further assess potential differential impacts and respond to the CIMM recommendation. A separate memorandum is being prepared to seek your approval to publish the results of the GBA Plus.
- The Department is moving forward with plans to deploy a cloud-based version of Chinook to replace the current Excel-based tool. This deployment aims to enhance the privacy and security of applicant information. As with earlier versions, the re-platformed version of Chinook will not automate or recommend decisions on client applications. Its functionality will mirror the current Excel-based functionality, providing decision-makers with a streamlined view of key information contained within client applications, in order to enhance productivity.
CONSIDERATIONS:

- Transparency: In response to calls for increased transparency about automation tools, and scrutiny regarding Chinook, specifically in CIMM 8, this AIA aims to clarify what the Chinook tool is and how it is used by officers to create efficiencies in processing. The release of the AIA gives the Department an avenue to demonstrate transparency and potentially mitigate reputational damage.
- Governance: The TBS Directive and associated requirement to complete an AIA is one piece of a larger governance framework IRCC has developed to ensure the responsible use of advanced analytics and automation. This framework helps ensure compliance with legal and privacy frameworks, the protection of Charter rights, meeting the Service Delivery Sector's Anti-Racism Commitments, meeting previous mandate letter commitments towards GBA Plus and bias analysis, and following IRCC's Policy Playbook on Automated Decision Support. IRCC has leveraged this governance framework to work towards the completion of the AIA.
- Public perception and reputational risks: Chinook does not utilize AI, nor advanced analytics for decision-making, and there are no built-in decision-making algorithms. In the absence of substantive publicly available information on Chinook, various incorrect narratives about Chinook have developed, many of which allege that Chinook is an automated decision-making tool powered by AI. Accordingly, Chinook is often associated with some of our actual automated decision support tools, and features heavily in public discourse about our uses of decision automation and AI. The results of the AIA may have a neutral response from stakeholders, while some may appreciate that IRCC has attempted to increase transparency about this tool. Maintaining public trust in IRCC's use of technology to support administrative decision-making will be particularly important as the Department advances Digital Platform Modernization (DPM), which will make analytics and automation more central to application processing.
