                           AFFIDAVIT OF ANDIE DAPONTE

BACKGROUND OF AFFIANT

2.     I have been employed with IRCC since 2009 as a member of the Foreign Service.
       I began my career as a Migration Officer, which included postings to Mexico City,
       Mexico; Ankara, Turkey; and Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates.

3.   During my postings abroad, I became heavily involved in the development of tools
     and innovations for visa processing, which culminated in the development of the
     Chinook Tool (“Chinook”) with a team of IRCC in-house experts.

4.   Since January 2018, I have been involved in the development of Chinook and,
     since my 2019 return to IRCC’s National Headquarters in Ottawa, I have been the
     project lead and subject matter expert for Chinook.

TEMPORARY RESIDENT VISA AND RELATED APPLICATION CASELOAD

5.   IRCC receives a significant number of overseas applications for temporary
     resident visas, work permits, and study permits (collectively, an “Application” or
     “Applications”) on an annual basis, a volume that generally increases every year.
     Based on my review of internal summaries, in 2011, IRCC received 1,041,518
     overseas temporary resident visa (“TRV”) applications, 148,337 overseas work
     permit (“WP”) applications, and 133,967 overseas student permit (“SP”)
     applications. In 2019, IRCC received 2,174,484 overseas TRV applications,
     366,019 overseas WP applications, and 431,497 overseas SP applications.

6.   I anticipate that Application final volumes will be lower in 2020 because of COVID-
     19 limitations. However, over the eight years from 2011 to 2019, IRCC has
     received an increase of approximately 109% in overseas TRV applications, 147%
     in overseas WP applications, and 222% in overseas SP applications.

7.   Applications are processed by migration officers working in IRCC migration offices
     located in Canada and throughout the world. Migration offices that are located
     abroad operate in Canadian embassies, high commissions, and consulates. I refer
     to a migration officer as a “Decision-Maker” in this Affidavit.

GLOBAL CASE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM

8.   The Global Case Management System (“GCMS”) is IRCC’s integrated worldwide
     electronic information management system used internally to process applications
     for citizenship, immigration, and certain passport services.
                                                                                         3


9.    In order to process Applications directly within the GCMS environment, Decision-
      Makers must review client and Application information contained within multiple
      screens. Each time Decision-Makers move to a different screen, they must wait for
      GCMS to load the information for that screen. Significant delays in Application
      processing result, particularly in migration offices with limited network bandwidth.

OVERVIEW OF CHINOOK

10.   In order to enhance efficiency and consistency, and to reduce processing times,
      IRCC implemented an in-house software-based tool in March 2018 to assist in
      processing Applications. This tool is known as Chinook.

11.   Chinook is a standalone, Microsoft Excel-based tool that functions in tandem with
      GCMS. It does not replace GCMS. Instead, it streamlines the administrative steps
      that would otherwise be required if Applications were processed directly within the
      GCMS environment.

12.   Application information is extracted from GCMS and presented within Chinook.
      Accordingly, Application information in Chinook is the same data as in GCMS.

13.   Chinook has a modular structure, in that processing tasks for Applications are
      divided into five modules, which are designated Module 1 (File Management),
      Module 2 (Pre-Assessment), Module 3 (Decision-Maker), Module 4 (Post-
      Decision), and Module 5 (Indicator Management).

14.   Decision-Makers are assigned a workload of Applications through Chinook. They
      are presented with the contents of assigned Applications on a single Excel
      spreadsheet (the “Module 3 Report”). This is to allow them to review contents of
      Applications on a single screen as opposed to multiple screens as would be
      required within the GCMS environment. This also enables them to complete
      administrative steps through batch processes.

15.   After reviewing a given Application using the Module 3 Report, Decision-Makers
      are able to prepare and record a decision, along with reasons if the Application is
                                                                                            4


      refused, within Chinook. Subsequently, the decision, any refusal reasons, and
      notes from Chinook are imported into GCMS, where they are retained.

16.   Evidence from migration offices generally reflects a 5% to 35% increase in
      Application processing volumes, depending on specific migration office, through
      the use of Chinook compared to the use of GCMS alone.

CHINOOK DEVELOPMENT

17.   Chinook was developed in-house by IRCC personnel who are subject matter
      experts in immigration processing or computer sciences. Specified personnel in
      IRCC’s International Network act as administrators over Chinook’s operation.

18.   Chinook was developed with the use of Microsoft Office products available within
      IRCC, in particular Microsoft Excel with “Visual Basic for Applications” coding.

19.   The initial version of Chinook was deployed in March 2018. Since that time,
      multiple iterations have been deployed as Chinook is subject to further updates
      and development. The most recent version update in primary use at migration
      offices occurred on July 6, 2020, which is the version described in this Affidavit.

20.   In May 2021, limited rollout of a version subsequent to the Chinook July 2020
      version commenced at certain migration offices. The Chinook May 2021 version
      has moved from reliance on Microsoft Excel to reliance on an online platform.
      IRCC intends to retain all functionality from the Chinook July 2020 version in the
      Chinook May 2021 version.

CHINOOK VERSION RELEVANT TO THE APPLICANT’S APPLICATION

THE FLOW OF APPLICATION DATA INTO CHINOOK

22.   Application data is populated or entered into GCMS from multiple channels,
      including, but not limited to, paper submission and a secure web portal.

23.   The Enterprise Data Warehouse (“EDW”) is an electronic corporate repository that
      houses various IRCC databases, including GCMS. It is relied on by IRCC as the
      source record for corporate information management. The EDW complies with all
      Government of Canada requirements for information technology security.

24.   Regardless of intake channel, Application data is populated or entered into GCMS
      and copied from GCMS to EDW each day. A copy is then automatically extracted
      from EDW and transmitted to the Chinook network drive every twelve hours.
      Chinook administrators retrieve the EDW extracts of Applications for which their
      region is responsible from the network drive and store those extracts on migration
      office local drives.

25.   Regional Chinook users then populate Chinook with Applications from their
      migration office local drives for processing and determination.

CHINOOK FUNCTIONALITY

Overview of Modules

26.   Chinook is organized into modules with each module adding a different layer of
      functionality to the tool. The five (5) modules are:

         a. Module 1: File Management – This module allows a user to self-assign open
             Applications based on the user’s processing tasks. For instance, an
             eligibility expert may assign herself open Applications that are ready for
             eligibility assessments. Module 1 is optional functionality and is not used at
             every migration office where Chinook has been implemented.

         b. Module 2: Pre-Assessment – This module presents a template to a program
             assistant for the completion of pre-assessment tasks, such as confirmation
             of supporting documents, verification of a client’s financial ability, and
             documentation of travel history. Pre-assessment notes from Module 2 are
                                                                                        6


            to be imported into GCMS’ Pre-Assessment Note field. After being
            populated in GCMS, the GCMS Pre-Assessment Note field is viewable by
            Decision-Makers in the Module 3 Report following the next EDW extraction.
            Module 2 is optional functionality and is not used at every migration office
            where Chinook has been implemented.

         c. Module 3: Decision-Maker – Decision-Makers use this module. The module
            presents Decision-Makers with the Module 3 Report, which provides the
            contents of assigned Applications in a single report. At this stage of
            processing, and outside of Chinook, Decision-Makers continue to have
            access to paper applications or electronic documents and GCMS, where
            Decision-Makers can review additional information and documentation from
            an Application or from GCMS. I further describe Module 3 below.

         d. Module 4: Post-Decision – Decision-Makers use this module. The module
            is presented as a “pop-up” dialogue window to Decision-Makers when they
            select a Column D data field, the “Action” field, in the Module 3 Report. The
            module records the decision and, if Decision-Makers refuse an Application
            after review, provides a notes generator to assist in the creation of reasons.
            I further describe Module 4 below.

         e. Module 5: Indicator Management – This module allows a Chinook user to
            submit requests to add, renew, or modify “risk indicators” and “local word
            flags”. A Chinook administrator is able to approve such requests through
            this module. Approved “risk indicators” and “local word flags” are then
            presented to Decision-Makers in Module 3, where applicable. I further
            describe Module 5 below.

Module 3: Decision-Maker

27.   As described above, Module 3 allows Decision-Makers to review the contents of
      assigned Applications in a single report, the Module 3 Report. Attached and
      marked as Exhibit “A” is a “pop-up” window that is presented to Decision-Makers
      upon loading Module 3 (1 page). An internal e-mail address has been redacted.
                                                                                             7


28.   The Module 3 Report contains rows and columns typical of a Microsoft Excel
      spreadsheet. Each row is divided into columns that range from A to AA. There are
      27 columns in total. The Module 3 Report primarily contains populated Application
      data, imported from the migration office local drive as discussed above, save for
      rows and columns that contain labels or digital control buttons.

29.   Each populated row represents one Application; multiple Applications are
      ordinarily displayed on separate rows on a single spreadsheet. Each populated
      column within a row presents data relevant to the corresponding Application, such
      as name, age, purpose of visit, date of receipt of the application, citizenship of the
      client, and previous travel.

30.   Attached and marked as Exhibit “B” is a mock-up of the Module 3 Report using
      simulated Application data (9 pages). The final two pages contain a reference to
      the spreadsheet cell and the contents that appear to the Decision-Maker in a “pop-
      up” note when placing the computer’s mouse cursor over the referenced cell.

31.   The columns are described as follows:

         a. Column A contains digital control buttons to interact with the Module 3
             Report and are not specific to a given Application.

         b. Columns B to C contain Application file and group number information
             populated from GCMS and which continues to be retained in GCMS.

         c. Column D reflects Decision-Makers’ decision on an Application. Decision-
             Makers are to “copy and paste” their decision into GCMS, where the
             outcome will be retained. When Decision-Makers select a data field in this
             column, Chinook presents the Module 4 “pop-up” dialogue window to record
             the decision and a notes generator if reasons for refusal are applicable. I
             describe Module 4 and the “copy and paste” process in more detail below.

         d. Column E is for working notes made by Decision-Makers while considering
             an Application. The column is intended to contain reminders or other
             transitory working notes. Entries in this column are not ordinarily retained.
                                                                                       8


         e. Columns F to Y contain Application information populated from GCMS, via
             the EDW extract from the migration office local drive, and which continues
             to be retained in GCMS. Column G contains any pre-assessment note by a
             program assistant in Module 2 and imported into GCMS.

         f. Columns Z to AA contain “risk indicators” and “local word flags,”
             respectively. This data is not from GCMS, but is based on indicators of
             interest from Module 5. Decision-Makers or other assigned Chinook users
             are to “copy and paste” these indicators into GCMS, where they will be
             retained. If there are no such indicators, Decision-Makers are to note that
             these are not applicable to an Application by recording “N/A” in GCMS. I
             describe “risk indicators” and “local word flags” as part of Module 5
             functionality and the “copy and paste” process in more detail below.

32.   Decision-Makers are also able to access documents in support of Applications
      independent of Chinook. The procedure to review supporting documents when
      processing an Application through Chinook remains the same as when an
      Application is processed on paper or directly within the GCMS environment.

Module 4: Post-Decision

33.   As described above, Module 4 appears as a “pop-up” dialogue window to Decision-
      Makers when they select Column D in the Module 3 Report. The module allows
      them to record a final decision to either approve or refuse an Application. They
      may also record a decision to request further information from a client, and may
      record that an Application is withdrawn by a client.

34.   If Decision-Makers refuse an Application after review, the module provides a notes
      generator to assist them in the creation of reasons.

35.   The notes generator presents standard language that Decision-Makers may
      select, review, and modify to fit the circumstances of an Application, in preparing
      reasons for refusal. Standard language is not automatically populated by the tool.
      Decision-Makers may choose not to use any standard language.
                                                                                          9


36.   If Decision-Makers choose to use standard language, the language may be
      modified to address further the specific circumstances of an Application and certain
      standard language selections require Decision-Makers to enter customized
      language. The standard language allows both positive and negative factors of an
      Application to be noted. The negative factors are linked to refusal grounds, as per
      the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act and the Immigration and Refugee
      Protection Regulations.

37.   Decision-Makers or other assigned Chinook users are to “copy and paste” any
      reasons for refusal from Module 4 into GCMS, where the reasons will be retained.
      I provide further detail on this process below.

Module 5: Indicator Management (Risk Indicators and Local Word Flags)

38.   As described above, Module 5 allows a Chinook user to submit requests to a
      Chinook administrator to add, renew, or modify “risk indicators” and “local word
      flags”. “Risk indicators” and “local word flags” are intended to assist Decision-
      Makers in their review of Applications.

Risk Indicators

39.   “Risk indicators” are used to notify Decision-Makers of trends that IRCC has
      detected, such as a trend that a falsified document was submitted by a certain
      company in a high number of Applications from different clients or otherwise to
      highlight a particular factor of concern.

40.   “Risk indicators” are also utilized to notify Decision-Makers of potentially low risk
      Applications; for example, if an international medical conference is being held in
      Canada, a “risk indicator” may be created to identify entry for such purpose to be
      of low risk to program integrity.

41.   “Risk indicators” may apply to all Applications or to a specific migration office. The
      inclusion of “risk indicators” within Chinook allows Decision-Makers to view
      applicable indicators in a centralized manner when determining an Application.
                                                                                        10


42.   An approved “risk indicator” within Chinook is linked to set criteria. For example, a
      “risk indicator” may be linked to a client’s declared occupation, such as “petroleum
      geologist”, or intended employer, such as “Acme Oil”, or a specified combination
      of criteria, such as “petroleum geologist” for “Acme Oil”.

43.   Approved “risk indicators” are presented in the Module 3 Report, along with a
      recommendation that Decision-Makers perform an activity in assessing an
      Application, such as a review of proof of credentials or an employment offer letter.
      The recommendation, however, does not direct Decision-Makers to arrive at any
      specific conclusion in conducting their assessment, but rather suggests steps to
      be taken to ascertain information.

Local Word Flags

44.   A “local word flag” is used to assist in triaging an Application in order to ensure
      priority processing of time-sensitive Applications, such as an Application to attend
      a wedding or a funeral.

45.   A “local word flag” is specific to a particular migration office. For example, the
      Beijing migration office may obtain approval from the Chinook administrator to
      include words associated to a wedding, such as “wedding”, “marriage”, or
      “ceremony”. The matched word found in any Application at the Beijing migration
      office is then presented in the Module 3 Report.

Indicator Management

46.   There is a process to create a “risk indicator” or “local word flag” within Chinook.
      An IRCC Risk Assessment Officer (“RAO”) or other approved user may submit
      requests to create such an indicator. A Chinook administrator then reviews
      requests for approval within Module 5. Each submission must be justified through
      rationale statements and are subject to modification or denial by the administrator.

47.   Following the above example, a RAO may find that a number of WP applications
      have included falsified letters of offer under the name of a specific company, such
                                                                                         11


      as “Acme Oil”. The RAO may then submit a request that the company name be
      included as a “risk indicator” due to concerns of falsified documentation.

48.   Chinook searches for “risk indicators” and “local word flags” in all Applications that
      are contained in a Module 3 Report. However, such indicators appear in the
      Module 3 Report only when they may be relevant to a particular Application.

49.   “Risk indicators” and “local word flags” are valid for four months from the date of
      approval, after which a Chinook administrator may renew or modify the indicator.

50.   As noted above, Decision-Makers or other assigned Chinook users are to “copy
      and paste” any “risk indicators” or “local word flags” presented in the Module 3
      Report into GCMS, where they will be retained. If there are no such indicators,
      Decision-Makers are to note that these are not applicable to an Application by
      recording “N/A” in GCMS. I expand on this process immediately below.

COMPLETION OF APPLICATION PROCESSING WITHIN CHINOOK

51.   Once Decision-Makers finalize decisions for all Applications in a given Module 3
      Report, they are to ensure that the decision, reasons, and any “risk indicators” or
      “local word flags” in the Module 3 Report are recorded in GCMS using the steps
      described in the paragraphs that follow.

52.   Decision-Makers are to click a button labelled “Action List” located within Column
      A of the Module 3 Report, which organizes data for ease of transfer into GCMS.
      The created “Action List” presents the decision, reasons for refusal if applicable,
      and any “risk indicators” or “local word flags” for each Application. If there were no
      “risk indicators” or “local word flags” associated with a given Application, then
      Decision-Makers must populate the corresponding GCMS “Notes” field with “N/A”
      to reflect that no such terms were present in the Module 3 Report.

53.   Decision-Makers are then required to “copy and paste” the final decision from
      Chinook into the “Final” field contained in GCMS. Decision-Makers, or assigned
      Chinook users on their behalf, are also required to “copy and paste” any reasons
                                                                                         12


      for decision and the field contents for “risk indicators” and “local word flags” from
      Chinook into the “Notes” field of GCMS.

54.   Accordingly, through this exportation process to GCMS and based on the
      Application data relationship described above between GCMS, EDW, and
      Chinook, all Application data present in the Module 3 Report, save for any
      transitory working notes in Column E, is retained in GCMS.

55.   Decision-Makers are to delete Module 3 Reports daily once the transfer of data
      into GCMS has been completed. The deletion of Module 3 Reports is to address
      privacy concerns, specifically duplicate data holdings, and operational concerns.

56.   A Privacy Risk Assessment prepared for IRCC in August 2019 identified privacy
      risks if multiple copies of client information were stored in various locations across
      IRCC and Global Affairs Canada networks or on local migration office
      workstations. IRCC Information Management and IT Operations have also
      concluded that the Module 3 Report is transitory in nature and, therefore, they
      recommended that the spreadsheet not be retained. Accordingly, IRCC has
      instructed Decision-Makers not to retain Module 3 Reports.

57.   From an operational perspective, as I have indicated, one purpose of Chinook is
      to increase processing efficiency of Applications. As such, Chinook was built to
      save Decision-Makers time in querying GCMS for Application information and to
      allow for the review of multiple Applications in a single Module 3 Report.

58.   If Decision-Makers were to save the associated Module 3 Report for each
      Application, this would require either a single Module 3 Report per Application or
      significant redactions to prevent inclusion of data from other clients. Both options
      would undermine efficiencies realized through the present Chinook design.

59.   In the first instance, this would result in the annual retention of approximately
      2,972,000 Module 3 Reports, based on 2019 Application volumes. It would also
      prevent the review of multiple Applications in a single Module 3 Report.
                                                                                         13


60.   In the second instance, this would require the creation of a library to retain a large
      number of Module 3 Reports. It would also require that IRCC staff locate, review,
      and redact the Module 3 Report associated to a particular Application to remove
      other client data prior to any external disclosure. For example, in a Module 3 Report
      containing 100 Applications, 99% of the report’s contents would require redaction.

      and for no other or improper purpose.
